California is not one roofing market

A roof in coastal Los Angeles, the Bay Area, the Central Valley and a mountain community can have very different labor, access and design constraints. The statewide number above is best treated as a first budget check. The contractor's measured roof area and written assembly matter more than the state average once you start collecting bids.

What is specifically worth checking in California in 2026

California's 2025 Title 24 building standards became effective in 2026, and the state publishes current code material through the Building Standards Commission. For homes in wildfire-exposed areas, CAL FIRE also publishes home-hardening guidance that treats the roof as a major ember and flame vulnerability.

Wildfire exposure changes the conversation

CAL FIRE recommends Class A-rated roof coverings and emphasizes closing gaps where embers can enter. That does not mean every California roof has the same wildfire scope. Ask the contractor to identify the requirement that applies to the address and to show the products and details used to satisfy it.

Heat, coast and mountain conditions are different jobs

An inland roof may put more emphasis on solar exposure and attic heat. Coastal projects can bring salt, moisture and corrosion considerations. Mountain locations can add snow, freeze-thaw and access issues. A useful proposal explains why the selected system fits the site instead of relying on a generic “California package.”

How to compare two California bids

  1. Confirm both contractors measured the same roof area.
  2. Compare the exact covering, underlayment and flashing system.
  3. Separate solar work, deck repair and permit fees.
  4. Check whether disposal, staging and property protection are included.
  5. Ask how wildfire or energy requirements were verified for the address.

California roof FAQ

Is the range a Los Angeles or San Francisco average?

No. It is a statewide planning baseline, deliberately broad because labor, access and permitting differ sharply by market.

Are solar removal costs included?

No. Roof-mounted equipment should have its own written scope, responsibility and warranty terms.

Does a Class A roof mean the home is wildfire-proof?

No. Roof covering is only one part of home hardening. Openings, vents, debris, siding transitions and surrounding vegetation also matter.
