Home Project Cost Examples

Worked hypothetical budgets showing how quantities, scope, allowances and contingency affect a project estimate.

Reviewed September 14, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Important: These examples are hypothetical planning exercises, not national price claims or completed customer projects. Replace every assumption with local measurements and quotes.

Example 1: Asphalt roof replacement

A homeowner estimates 1,850 square feet of roof surface and adds 10% waste, producing 2,035 square feet or 20.35 roofing squares. The budget separates tear-off, installed roofing system, permit allowance and possible decking sheets. A 12% contingency is held outside the base scope for concealed damage. This structure makes local bids easier to compare than multiplying living area by one price.

Example 2: Interior room painting

A 12-by-14-foot room with an 8-foot ceiling has 416 square feet of gross wall area before subtracting doors and windows. Two finish coats may require more than one gallon depending on product coverage, color change and surface. The budget lists preparation, primer if needed, wall paint, trim paint, supplies and labor separately.

Example 3: Concrete patio

A 12-by-16-foot patio at 4 inches thick requires about 2.37 cubic yards before waste: 12 × 16 × 0.333 feet ÷ 27. Adding 10% produces roughly 2.61 cubic yards. The full budget also considers excavation, base, reinforcement, forms, delivery minimums, finishing and access.

Example 4: Flooring replacement

A measured 1,000 square feet of floor area with 8% waste requires approximately 1,080 square feet of material. The project budget can include removal, disposal, subfloor preparation, underlayment, transitions, trim and furniture handling. A low material price alone does not describe installed scope.

Example 5: HVAC replacement

An HVAC budget begins with qualified load and system evaluation, not house area alone. Equipment efficiency, duct condition, electrical work, permits, controls and access affect the proposal. Homeowners can compare options using the same comfort requirements and documented inclusions.

Turn an example into your plan

  1. Replace sample dimensions with measurements.
  2. List every included and excluded task.
  3. Obtain local unit prices or itemized proposals.
  4. Add allowances only for undecided selections.
  5. Keep contingency separate from base scope.
  6. Record revisions as the project develops.
