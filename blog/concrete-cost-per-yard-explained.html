Concrete Cost Per Yard: What the Quote Really Includes

The supplier's price per cubic yard is only one part of a concrete project budget.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

The quoted price per cubic yard is the price of one part of a concrete project: the ready-mix material. A homeowner can receive an accurate material quote and still underestimate the project if delivery, short-load fees, pumping, reinforcement, forms and finishing are missing.

Build the quote in layers

Cost layerWhat to ask for
Ready-mixPrice per cubic yard for the specified mix
DeliveryIncluded radius, fuel/delivery charge and waiting time
Short loadMinimum order or charge below a truck threshold
PlacementDirect chute, pump, buggy or additional labor
Site workExcavation, base, forms, reinforcement and access
FinishStrike-off, finishing, joints, curing and cleanup

Worked planning example

Imagine a project requiring 4.5 cubic yards after quantity planning. If the supplier quotes a per-yard material rate, multiply that rate by 4.5 for the ready-mix subtotal only. Then add any delivery, short-load, pumping or waiting charges separately. This prevents the common mistake of treating “price per yard” as the installed project price.

Mix design changes the material quote

Strength, aggregate, slump, air entrainment, fibers, admixtures and exposure requirements can affect price. The correct mix should be specified for the actual project rather than selected only by price.

Short-load charges can matter on small jobs

Batch plants and trucks have fixed operating costs, so a small order can carry a minimum or short-load charge. Ask for the delivered total, not just the per-yard rate.

Access can change placement cost

If the truck cannot discharge near the forms, the project may need a pump, buggy or more labor. Confirm truck access, hose or boom reach, staging and washout before pour day.

Installed cost includes site work

Excavation, compacted base, vapor barrier where applicable, reinforcement, forms, edge thickening, joints, finishing and curing can all be separate from the ready-mix price.

Questions to ask a ready-mix supplier

  1. What is the current price for the specified mix?
  2. What order size triggers a short-load fee?
  3. What delivery radius is included?
  4. Are fuel or environmental fees separate?
  5. What waiting-time policy applies?
  6. What are the minimum order increments?
  7. Do I need to arrange pumping separately?

Use a local price in the calculator

Enter the supplier's current quote into the Concrete Calculator. That keeps the quantity math and the local market price separate, which is more transparent than relying on a static national cost claim.

FAQ

Is concrete sold only by the cubic yard?

Ready-mix is commonly quoted by cubic yard, but the delivered invoice can also contain fixed or service charges.

Does price per yard include labor?

Usually not. Placement and finishing labor are separate unless a contractor's proposal explicitly includes them.

Why can two suppliers quote different totals?

Mix design, distance, minimums, fees and service assumptions may differ. Compare the complete delivered scope.
