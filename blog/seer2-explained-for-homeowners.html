SEER2 Explained for Homeowners

SEER2 is useful for comparing cooling efficiency, but it is not a complete measure of system quality, sizing or installed performance.

Updated September 9, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Homeowners often see SEER2 highlighted in HVAC proposals. It is an efficiency rating based on seasonal cooling performance under standardized testing, not a prediction of your exact utility bill.

What SEER2 measures

SEER2 relates seasonal cooling output to electrical energy used under a standardized test procedure. A higher number generally indicates greater rated cooling efficiency.

Why SEER2 is not the whole system

Duct leakage, incorrect refrigerant charge, airflow problems, oversized equipment and poor installation can reduce real-world performance even when the equipment has a strong rating.

Compare matched equipment

Ask for exact indoor and outdoor model numbers and confirm the combination is rated together. Comparing only the outdoor-unit label can be misleading.

Climate and usage still matter

Cooling hours, thermostat settings, humidity, insulation and local electricity prices affect operating cost.
