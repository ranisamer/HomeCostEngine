Window Replacement Cost Per Window: What Should Be Included?

A per-window price is only meaningful when the product and installation scope are clearly defined.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

“Cost per window” sounds simple until you compare two proposals and discover that one is an insert replacement while the other removes the old frame, repairs the opening, flashes the exterior and replaces trim. The number is useful only after the installation method and product are defined.

Build a window schedule before collecting bids

Give every bidder the same list of openings. Number the windows and record approximate size, operating type, room and any obvious damage around the opening. That reduces the chance that one contractor prices 14 units while another quietly counts 13.

Insert replacement and full-frame replacement are different jobs

An insert unit preserves more of the existing frame and trim. Full-frame replacement exposes more of the opening and can include sill repair, flashing, insulation, exterior trim and interior finish work. Neither method is automatically right for every home; the condition of the existing frame and the project goal matter.

Product specifications can move price before labor changes

  • Frame material and construction
  • Operating style and hardware
  • Glass package and whole-window ratings
  • Tempered or safety glazing where required
  • Grids, colors and interior/exterior finishes
  • Special shapes and large units

Opening condition is where surprises happen

Rotten sill material, old water damage, out-of-square openings or damaged siding can turn a straightforward replacement into repair work. Ask the proposal to state how concealed damage is documented and whether repairs use a unit price, allowance or change order.

Do not forget the finish work

A quote can stop at “window installed” while leaving caulk, interior casing, stool/apron work, exterior trim, siding repair, drywall or paint to someone else. Those exclusions should be visible before you compare totals.

Questions that make per-window pricing meaningful

  1. What exact product and glass package is included?
  2. Is the method insert or full-frame?
  3. How is the opening flashed and air-sealed?
  4. Who handles interior and exterior trim?
  5. How are damaged sills or framing priced?
  6. Are disposal and permit costs included?
  7. What manufacturer and workmanship warranties apply?

Energy performance: compare the whole product

Use the NFRC label and climate-appropriate ENERGY STAR criteria when energy performance is part of the decision. Pane count alone is not enough; compare U-factor and SHGC for the actual rated unit.
