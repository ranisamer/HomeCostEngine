Furnace Replacement Cost: What to Budget in 2026

Understand furnace replacement cost in 2026, including equipment size, efficiency, fuel, venting, electrical work, ductwork and installation scope.

Published September 11, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Furnace replacement cost is affected by more than the equipment price. Capacity, efficiency, fuel type, venting, electrical work, controls, duct transitions, permits and access all shape the installed proposal. A good budget begins with the full replacement scope and a qualified load calculation.

Start with the heating load

Do not assume the replacement must match the old furnace capacity. Insulation, windows, air leakage and previous sizing choices can affect the correct load. Use the HVAC Replacement Cost Calculator for an early planning range, then have a qualified contractor determine final sizing.

Equipment efficiency and type

Higher-efficiency equipment can require different venting, condensate drainage or electrical work. Gas, oil and electric systems also have different installation requirements. Compare complete systems rather than assuming the furnace cabinet is the only cost.

Venting and combustion safety

A replacement may need chimney inspection, new vent material, combustion-air provisions or changes for a condensing furnace. These are safety-critical details. Ask how the contractor will test operation and verify venting after installation.

Ductwork and airflow

A new furnace cannot correct every comfort problem if return air, supply ducts or registers are inadequate. Ask whether the proposal includes inspection of static pressure, filtration and duct transitions. For a broader house-size example, see the HVAC replacement guide for a 2,000 sq ft house.

Repair or replace?

Age alone does not decide the question. Consider the failed component, repair history, reliability, safety, comfort and the condition of the rest of the system. Use the HVAC repair versus replacement guide to structure the comparison.

Compare a furnace with a heat pump

In some climates and homes, a heat pump or dual-fuel system may be considered alongside a furnace. The correct comparison includes heating and cooling scope, electrical capacity, climate performance and backup heat. Review heat pump versus central air cost as a starting point.

What a complete quote should show

Request the equipment model, efficiency rating, capacity, thermostat, filter, venting, drain, electrical scope, duct transitions, permits, disposal, commissioning and warranty. The HVAC budgeting guide can help you compare the assumptions behind multiple proposals.

Planning note: HomeCostEngine estimates are for budgeting and education. Verify measurements, product requirements, permits, code requirements and contractor scope with qualified local professionals.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace a furnace with the same size?

Not automatically. Final capacity should be based on the home’s current heating load and the complete system.

What is often excluded from a furnace quote?

Possible exclusions include duct repairs, electrical upgrades, venting changes, permits, asbestos work, thermostat and condensate drainage.

Can a heat pump replace a furnace?

Sometimes, depending on climate, home performance, electrical capacity and system design. A qualified local contractor should evaluate the options.
