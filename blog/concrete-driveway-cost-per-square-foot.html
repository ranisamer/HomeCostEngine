Concrete Driveway Cost Per Square Foot: 2026 Planning Guide

Estimate concrete driveway cost per square foot by understanding thickness, base preparation, reinforcement, finish, access and ready-mix delivery.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Quick answer: a driveway's installed cost per square foot is the result of demolition, excavation, base preparation, slab design, ready-mix, reinforcement, finishing, access and cleanup. The concrete itself is only one part of the job.

Separate the project into cost layers

LayerWhat belongs there
RemovalOld concrete/asphalt demolition and disposal
EarthworkExcavation, grading and unsuitable-soil correction
BaseAggregate material, placement and compaction
ConcreteVolume, mix, delivery and placement
ReinforcementRebar, mesh or other specified system
Finish / jointsBroom or decorative finish, control joints and curing
Site logisticsPumping, limited access, protection and cleanup

Thickness changes cubic yards, not just a line item

For an 800 sq ft driveway, moving from 4 inches to 5 inches increases theoretical concrete volume by 25%. Use the Concrete Calculator to see the volume effect before comparing a contractor's price.

A low square-foot number can hide weak base preparation

Ask how much material is being removed, what base is installed, compacted depth, how soft spots are handled and whether grading/drainage corrections are included. Those details can matter more to the job than a small difference in ready-mix price.

Access changes placement cost

If the ready-mix truck cannot discharge near the forms, the project may need extra labor, buggies or a pump. Delivery minimums and waiting-time charges can also affect smaller or complex placements.

Decorative work should be isolated from the structural base price

Color, exposed aggregate, borders, saw-cut patterns and specialty sealers can add material and labor. Separate them so you can compare the underlying driveway construction first.

How to compare two driveway quotes

  1. Confirm same measured area.
  2. Confirm same slab and edge thickness.
  3. Compare excavation and base preparation.
  4. Compare reinforcement and joint plan.
  5. Compare finish and curing.
  6. Confirm disposal, permits and cleanup.

Read Concrete Driveway Thickness and How Much Extra Concrete Should I Order? for the quantity side of the job.

Best use of a square-foot price: normalize bids only after the driveway construction scope is the same.
