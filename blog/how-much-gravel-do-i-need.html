How Much Gravel Do I Need?

Gravel quantity starts with volume, but suppliers often sell stone by weight. You need both numbers to plan confidently.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Gravel planning starts with volume, but ordering usually ends with either cubic yards or tons. The most reliable workflow is to calculate the space first, then confirm the supplier's product-specific conversion before placing the order.

Quick formula: cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27. If depth is entered in inches, divide it by 12 first.

Worked example: a 40 × 10 ft gravel area

Suppose a path or parking strip measures 40 feet by 10 feet and you plan a 3-inch layer. Three inches is 0.25 feet.

StepCalculationResult
Area40 × 10400 sq ft
Volume400 × 0.25100 cu ft
Cubic yards100 ÷ 273.70 yd³
5% planning allowance3.70 × 1.053.89 yd³

This example gives you a volume target, not a guaranteed delivered tonnage. Crushed stone, pea gravel, river rock and limestone can have different bulk densities, moisture levels and supplier gradations.

When to use cubic yards and when to use tons

Use cubic yards when you are estimating how much space the material must fill. Use tons when the supplier sells by weight. Do not assume a universal tons-per-yard conversion; ask the supplier what conversion they use for the exact product you are buying.

The right depth depends on the use, base condition, drainage, compaction and expected loads. A decorative bed should not be planned the same way as a driveway base or a load-bearing surface.

What can make your first estimate wrong?

  • Uneven excavation or an irregular site
  • Compaction after placement
  • Material spilled during handling
  • Existing low spots that require extra fill
  • Supplier minimum-order increments
  • Using a generic weight conversion for a different stone product

Use the gravel calculator, then call the supplier

The Gravel Calculator converts area and depth into cubic yards and gives a planning weight estimate. Before ordering, tell the supplier your calculated cubic yards, project use and desired depth. Ask them to confirm the recommended tonnage for their current stock.

Ordering checklist

  1. Measure the actual footprint, including widening or turnouts.
  2. Confirm the designed material depth after base preparation.
  3. Calculate cubic yards.
  4. Add only a reasonable project-specific allowance.
  5. Confirm product, gradation and supplier weight conversion.
  6. Check delivery access and truck placement before scheduling.

FAQ

Should I always add 10%?

No. A simple rectangular area with good measurements may need less than an irregular excavation with uncertain depth. Treat waste as a planning allowance, not a fixed rule.

Can I convert cubic yards to tons myself?

You can create a rough estimate, but the supplier's actual product density is more useful. The same cubic-yard volume can weigh differently depending on stone type, size distribution and moisture.

Is driveway gravel calculated the same as landscape gravel?

The volume math is the same, but the layer build-up and product choice can be very different. Verify the base and surface design for the intended use.
