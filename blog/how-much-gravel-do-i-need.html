How Much Gravel Do I Need?

Gravel quantity starts with volume, but suppliers often sell stone by weight. You need both numbers to plan confidently.

Updated September 7, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Start by measuring the area in square feet and choosing the intended gravel depth. Multiply area by depth to get volume, then convert that volume to cubic yards.

Why tons and cubic yards are different

Cubic yards measure volume. Tons measure weight. The conversion depends on stone type, particle size, moisture and supplier grading, so a tons-per-yard factor is always an approximation.

Use the gravel calculator

The Gravel Calculator estimates cubic yards and tons for pea gravel, crushed stone, river rock and limestone gravel. You can also enter a local price per ton.

How deep should gravel be?

The appropriate depth depends on use and base preparation. A decorative bed is different from a driveway or load-bearing surface. Verify the project build-up before ordering material.

Confirm the supplier conversion

After calculating, ask your supplier how many tons they recommend for the calculated cubic-yard volume. Their product-specific bulk density should be more accurate than a generic factor.
