How Much Flooring Waste Should I Add?

The measured floor area is rarely the exact amount of material you should order.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Quick answer: there is no single waste percentage that fits every flooring job. The right allowance depends on room shape, product dimensions, layout direction, pattern, cuts and the amount of spare material you want to keep.

Keep three quantities separate

QuantityWhat it means
Measured areaActual floor area receiving the product
Installation allowanceExtra for cuts, layout and unusable pieces
Future-repair stockMaterial intentionally saved after installation

Why simple rooms usually need less extra

A rectangular room with a straight plank layout typically creates fewer offcuts than several small rooms, many doorways or a diagonal installation. Patterned floors such as herringbone can create substantially more cutting because end pieces are harder to reuse.

Product dimensions matter

Large tiles, long planks and products with fixed visual patterns can create offcuts that cannot be reused elsewhere. Natural variation can also cause some pieces to be set aside for appearance. Use the exact product and layout—not just the material category—to choose the allowance.

Package size affects the final order

After calculating the purchase target, divide by the exact square-foot coverage per box or carton and round up to a whole package. That rounding may already create some extra material, so do not automatically add another large buffer without checking the result.

Worked example

A project measures 850 sq ft. With an 8% installation allowance, the target becomes 918 sq ft. If each box covers 23.5 sq ft, 918 ÷ 23.5 = 39.06, so the order becomes 40 boxes. The package rounding raises the actual purchased coverage above the 918 sq ft target.

Keep spare stock on purpose

If you want matching material for future repairs, decide on that quantity separately and store it dry and labeled. A later production run may vary slightly in color or finish, and the exact product may eventually be discontinued.

Ask the installer for the reason behind the allowance

If a contractor recommends a higher percentage, ask what in the layout or product creates the extra cuts. A transparent explanation is more useful than “we always use 10%.”

Use the Flooring Cost Calculator to test different allowances and How Much Flooring Do I Need? for the full order workflow.

Better rule: measured area first, justified cut allowance second, whole-package rounding third, repair stock last.
