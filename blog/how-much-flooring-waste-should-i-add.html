How Much Flooring Waste Should I Add?

The measured floor area is rarely the exact amount of material you should order.

Reviewed October 24, 2026 • By HomeCostEngine Editorial

Quick answer: choose extra flooring from the exact product and layout, not a universal percentage. First measure every area receiving the floor. Add a justified allowance for cuts and unusable pieces, round to whole cartons, then add any unopened repair stock you deliberately want to keep. The percentages below are hypothetical scenarios, not manufacturer recommendations.

Use the flooring material-and-labor calculator to compare purchase-area assumptions. Keep the installer's layout and the supplier's exact carton coverage beside the result; a material-cost estimate is not a complete order or an installation specification.

Keep the four parts of an order separate

PartHow to record itWhat it does not cover
Measured installed areaRooms, halls and closets actually receiving this productAn omitted room is not waste
Installation allowanceExtra for justified cuts, layout and unusable piecesUnpriced subfloor repairs or extra rooms
Carton roundingRound the installation purchase target up to full packagesA guarantee of usable matching repair pieces
Reserved repair stockFull cartons or agreed usable pieces deliberately left after installationMaterial the installer needs to finish the job

Installation purchase target = measured area × (1 + allowance as a decimal). Installation cartons = round up(target ÷ coverage per carton). If you require one unopened repair carton, add it after calculating those installation cartons. Do not subtract planned cutting loss from the installed area or add the same allowance twice.

Ask what creates the cuts

A useful allowance explanation names the actual constraint: diagonal cuts at the boundary, a directional pattern, narrow final rows, short returns at doorways, or damaged or unsuitable pieces that cannot be installed. Ask for a room sketch with the starting wall and laying direction. Two rooms with the same square footage can produce different offcuts because their widths interact differently with the plank or tile dimensions.

Offcuts are reusable only when they still meet the product's installation requirements. A piece may have enough area but lack the correct locking edge, required end length or pattern position. Do not count every cut piece as reusable stock. Ask the installer whether compatible rooms can share offcuts without violating the layout instructions; separate products, sizes or patterns need separate takeoffs.

Use the exact product instructions before approving a percentage. Pergo's installation resources direct buyers to measure the space and review instructions by product line. They do not establish one percentage for every floor. Carpet sold in rolls also needs a width, seam and pattern-repeat plan; it cannot be ordered by applying this carton formula alone.

Worked example: 850 square feet and 23.5-square-foot cartons

Suppose the verified installed area is 850 sq ft and the installer proposes an illustrative 8% allowance for a particular straight layout. The target is 850 × 1.08 = 918 sq ft. Divide by a hypothetical 23.5 sq ft per carton: 918 ÷ 23.5 = 39.0638. Round up to 40 cartons, providing 940 sq ft.

The order includes 68 sq ft of planned installation allowance plus 22 sq ft from packaging. Purchased coverage exceeds the measured floor by 90 sq ft, or 10.59%. That does not mean 90 sq ft will remain after installation. Some will become cuts, and the remaining pieces may not be suitable for a later repair.

The 15% scenario needs two additional cartons. Your actual layout may require a different amount; this table demonstrates rounding, not the correct allowance for your home.

Missing area is a measurement correction

If a 40 sq ft hall was omitted, the measured area becomes 890 sq ft. At the same illustrative 8%, the target is 961.2 sq ft, requiring 41 cartons and providing 963.5 sq ft. Do not leave the area at 850 and hope the waste buffer covers the hall. That would consume material already reserved for installation cuts.

The separate room-by-room flooring order guide explains how to combine compatible measured areas before carton rounding. This page focuses on why the allowance exists and how to audit it, so the same room should never appear in both the measurement and an extra-area adjustment.

Specify repair stock before signing

For the original 850 sq ft example, one deliberately unopened repair carton means 41 cartons total: 40 allocated to installation plus one reserved carton. Total coverage is 963.5 sq ft. The reserved carton is separate from whatever scraps or partial boards remain. Tell the installer which carton is reserved, and agree how any unexpected shortage will be approved before opening it.

Confirm matching product identification and any shade or batch details the manufacturer uses. Follow its storage instructions, retain the label and receipt, and ask about unopened-carton return deadlines and restocking terms. Do not assume future availability or a right to return surplus. Product choices, supplier stock and local delivery schedules can change the practical ordering decision.

Check the budget and exclusions separately

Extra material does not price demolition, disposal, moisture correction, subfloor leveling, transitions, stairs, trim or furniture moving. Shaw's installation preparation guidance recommends confirming installer responsibilities. Compare complete scope using the installed flooring cost breakdown, keeping purchased material area separate from the area used to price labor.

Record the measured area, product code, carton coverage, approved allowance, installation cartons and reserved stock in the free home-project planning workbook. Return to the calculator when the layout or product changes. A different carton coverage can change the order even when the measured floor stays the same. Local permit, building or association requirements should be checked if the work extends into structural repairs or other regulated scope.

Frequently asked questions

Is 10% extra flooring always enough?

No. The exact product, room dimensions, layout, pattern and permitted offcut reuse determine the allowance. Ask the installer to explain the quantity using the selected product instructions and layout.

Does carton rounding count as installation allowance?

Whole-carton rounding adds coverage after the allowance is calculated. Record that surplus separately; it is not automatically an unopened carton for future repairs.

Should an omitted closet be covered by the waste percentage?

No. Add every area receiving the flooring to the measured area first. Known additional floor area is installed work, not cutting waste.

How do I keep one unopened repair carton?

Calculate and round the installation requirement first, then add the number of full cartons you deliberately want to keep unopened. Confirm the plan with the installer before ordering.
