How Much Flooring Waste Should I Add?

The measured floor area is rarely the exact amount of material you should order.

Updated September 7, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Flooring requires cuts around walls, doorways, cabinets and irregular shapes. Some boards or tiles may be damaged, and patterned layouts can create additional offcuts. That is why most project plans include a waste allowance above measured square footage.

Start with measured floor area

Measure each room carefully and total the areas that will actually receive flooring. Then use our Flooring Cost Calculator to test different waste percentages, material prices and labor assumptions.

Why waste percentages vary

Simple rectangular rooms laid with straightforward plank flooring generally create less waste than diagonal layouts, herringbone patterns or rooms with many angles. Natural materials may also require more selection for appearance or defects.

Do not forget packaging increments

Flooring is commonly sold by the carton, box or bundle rather than by exact square foot. Your final purchase quantity therefore needs to be rounded to the supplier's package size.

Keep some material after installation

A small amount of matching material can be useful for future repairs. Product colors and finishes can change between manufacturing runs, and the exact line may eventually be discontinued.

Compare installed quotes carefully

Ask whether a flooring quote includes old-floor removal, subfloor preparation, leveling, underlayment, transitions, baseboards, stairs, delivery and furniture moving. Use your material calculation to understand which parts of the quote are driven by quantity and which are separate services.
