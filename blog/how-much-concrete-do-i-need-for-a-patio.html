How Much Concrete Do I Need for a Patio?

Concrete is ordered by volume, so patio length and width are only part of the calculation.

Updated September 7, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

To estimate concrete for a patio, you need three dimensions: length, width and slab thickness. Multiply them to get volume, convert the result to cubic yards, then add a sensible waste allowance for real-world variation.

Use the concrete calculator

The fastest method is our Concrete Calculator. Enter the patio length and width in feet, thickness in inches and a waste percentage. The tool returns cubic yards and an approximate bag count.

Why slab thickness matters

A small change in thickness can significantly change volume across a large patio. Do not assume every patio should use the same thickness. Soil, loading, reinforcement, climate and local practice can affect the design, so structural decisions should be verified with a qualified professional.

Why add a waste allowance?

Forms are not always perfect, the base may be uneven, some material can be lost during placement and ready-mix suppliers may have ordering increments. Ordering exactly the theoretical volume can leave you short at the worst possible time.

Bulk concrete vs bags

Bagged concrete can make sense for small pours or repairs. Larger patios are often easier to place with ready-mix delivery, but minimum-load and short-load fees can affect the economics. Use the calculator result to ask suppliers for comparable delivered pricing.

Plan the whole project

Concrete quantity is only one part of the budget. Base preparation, excavation, reinforcement, forms, finishing, control joints, delivery and labor may be separate costs. Treat material volume as the starting point, not the entire project estimate.
