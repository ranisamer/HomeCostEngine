How Much Concrete Do I Need for a Patio?

Concrete is ordered by volume, so patio length and width are only part of the calculation.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Quick answer: patio concrete is calculated from length × width × thickness, then converted to cubic yards. Theoretical volume is only the first step; thickened edges, uneven base conditions and supplier order increments can change the practical quantity.

The formula

Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (ft) ÷ 27. If thickness is entered in inches, divide by 12 first.

Worked example: 12 × 16 ft patio at 4 inches

Four inches equals 0.333 ft. Multiply 12 × 16 × 0.333 to get about 63.9 cubic feet. Divide by 27 and the theoretical volume is about 2.37 cubic yards.

How thickness changes the order

12 × 16 ft patioTheoretical volume
3 in1.78 yd³
4 in2.37 yd³
5 in2.96 yd³
6 in3.56 yd³

Do not choose thickness from the quantity table

Thickness should reflect intended use, soil support, base preparation, reinforcement, climate and local requirements. The table converts an approved thickness into volume; it does not design the slab.

Calculate thickened edges and steps separately

If the patio has a perimeter beam, footing, step or ramp, calculate those shapes independently and add them to the slab volume. Do not hide known extra geometry inside a “waste” percentage.

Base and concrete are different materials

If the project uses a compacted aggregate base, calculate it separately. Four inches of base plus four inches of concrete does not mean eight inches of concrete.

Ready-mix or bags?

For small pours, bagged material can be practical. As volume grows, mixing speed, consistency and labor become more important. Ready-mix can simplify placement, but minimum-load, short-load and delivery fees should be confirmed with the supplier.

Before you order

  1. Measure the inside of the forms.
  2. Check depth at several points.
  3. Calculate any thickened sections separately.
  4. Choose a justified quantity allowance.
  5. Confirm supplier minimums and delivery increments.
  6. Make sure labor, access and finishing tools are ready.

Use the Concrete Calculator for exact dimensions and How Much Extra Concrete Should I Order? for allowance planning.

Planning takeaway: the correct order starts with field dimensions and the approved slab section, not a generic patio size.
