HVAC Replacement Cost for a 1,500 Sq Ft House

Plan HVAC replacement for a 1,500 sq ft house using system type, efficiency, sizing, ductwork and local labor assumptions.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

A 1,500-square-foot home is only a starting point for HVAC budgeting. Final equipment selection depends on the home's actual heating and cooling load, climate, insulation, windows, air leakage, duct system and installation scope.

Use floor area for budgeting, not final sizing

Square footage can help you create an early budget, but it should not be used as the only equipment-sizing method. Ask the contractor how load calculations were performed and what indoor/outdoor design conditions were assumed.

Build the estimate in layers

Cost layerWhat to verify
EquipmentExact indoor/outdoor model numbers and capacity
EfficiencyMatched-system rating, not one component alone
DuctworkReuse, sealing, transitions, repairs or replacement
ElectricalDisconnect, breaker, circuit or service changes
Refrigerant lineReuse, flush, resize or replace
Drain/condensateDrain routing, pan, pump and safety switches
ControlsThermostat, communicating controls or zoning
Permits/startupPermit, inspection, evacuation, charging and commissioning

Why two 1,500 sq ft homes can need different systems

A shaded, well-insulated home with tight construction can have a very different load from a similar-size home with high solar exposure, leaky windows, high ceilings or poor insulation. This is why floor area is not a reliable substitute for a proper load calculation.

Check the duct system before assuming it can stay

Existing ducts may have leakage, undersized returns, restrictive filters or poor room balance. Ask whether the proposal includes static-pressure checks, sealing, resizing or new transitions.

Compare complete matched systems

For split systems, efficiency and performance can depend on the combination of indoor unit, outdoor unit and controls. Compare the complete matched system rather than only the outdoor model.

Create three budget scenarios

  1. Basic replacement: equipment swap with compatible existing infrastructure.
  2. Moderate scope: equipment plus duct sealing, electrical or line-set work.
  3. Complex scope: substantial duct modifications, zoning, access work or system conversion.

Questions to ask each bidder

  • How was capacity selected?
  • What exact models are included?
  • What ductwork is included or excluded?
  • What electrical and refrigerant-line work is included?
  • How is startup and commissioning documented?
  • What labor and equipment warranties apply?
