HVAC Replacement Cost for a 1,500 Sq Ft House

Plan HVAC replacement for a 1,500 sq ft house using system type, efficiency, sizing, ductwork and local labor assumptions.

Updated September 8, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

For a 1,500-square-foot home, HVAC replacement cost depends on far more than floor area. System type, correct load sizing, efficiency, duct condition, electrical work, controls, permits and the local labor market all change the final proposal.

Start with a planning range

Use the HVAC Replacement Cost Calculator and enter 1,500 square feet as the starting home size. Test central AC, heat pump, furnace and ductless options separately instead of assuming they have the same installed scope.

Square footage is not equipment sizing

A 1,500 sq ft house can have very different heating and cooling loads depending on climate, insulation, windows, orientation, air leakage and ceiling height. A contractor should perform an appropriate load calculation before final equipment selection.

Compare the complete scope

Ask each bidder to identify indoor and outdoor model numbers, thermostat or controls, refrigerant-line work, electrical work, drain work, permits, disposal, startup, testing and warranty terms. A lower headline price may simply include less.

Ductwork can change the budget

If the existing ducts leak, are undersized or need transitions, replacement cost can rise. Make sure duct repairs or modifications are shown separately so you can compare bids consistently.

Efficiency affects more than equipment price

Higher-efficiency equipment can cost more up front and may require compatible indoor components or controls. Compare installed scope and expected operating characteristics rather than buying only from an efficiency label.

Use two or three bids after calculating

The calculator gives you a budgeting baseline. Then compare multiple local proposals against the same written scope. Also read How to Budget for HVAC Replacement and HVAC Repair vs Replacement.

Planning note: HomeCostEngine estimates are for budgeting. Verify final measurements, product requirements, permits, code requirements and contractor scope locally before purchasing or hiring.

Frequently asked questions

Can I size HVAC from 1,500 square feet alone?

No. Floor area is only a rough planning input; final equipment sizing should consider the home's actual heating and cooling load.

Should ductwork be included in an HVAC quote?

The proposal should clearly state whether duct repairs, transitions, sealing or replacement are included or excluded.

What is the best first step?

Create a planning range with the HVAC calculator, then request like-for-like local proposals.
