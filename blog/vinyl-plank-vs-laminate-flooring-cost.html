Vinyl Plank vs Laminate Flooring Cost: What Should You Compare?

Compare vinyl plank and laminate flooring by material price, installation, subfloor prep, waste, moisture needs and total installed scope.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Quick answer: vinyl plank and laminate can both work as budget-conscious floating floors, but the better value depends on the exact product, subfloor condition, moisture exposure, trim work and installation method. Compare complete installed scope—not the box price.

What actually changes the total

Cost driverVinyl plankLaminate
Product constructionThickness, wear layer, attached pad, locking profileCore construction, wear rating, attached pad, locking profile
Subfloor prepFlatness and moisture conditions still matterFlatness and moisture conditions still matter
UnderlaymentMay be built in or specified separatelyOften product/system specific

Subfloor condition can matter more than the material choice

If the floor is out of flat, damaged or has a moisture problem, both systems can require extra preparation. Ask the installer to state the assumed floor condition and how leveling, patching or moisture mitigation would be priced.

Do not compare products by thickness alone

A thicker plank is not automatically a better floor. Look at the complete specification, warranty requirements, wear surface, locking system, approved substrate and installation instructions for the exact product.

Moisture claims need product-level verification

“Waterproof” and “water-resistant” are marketing terms that can mean different things in practice. Check the manufacturer’s current installation and warranty documents for wet-area restrictions, perimeter sealing, vapor-barrier requirements and what happens after standing water.

Waste depends on layout

A simple straight layout in rectangular rooms generally creates fewer offcuts than diagonal installation, patterned layouts or rooms with many closets and doorways. Keep measured area separate from waste so the purchase quantity is transparent.

Run the same example through both options

For a 900 sq ft project with an 8% purchase allowance, you would price 972 sq ft of material for either option. Then add each product's actual underlayment, transitions, preparation and labor. That makes the comparison about the system, not a headline price.
