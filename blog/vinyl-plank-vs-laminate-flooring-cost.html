Vinyl Plank vs Laminate Flooring Cost: What Should You Compare?

Compare vinyl plank and laminate flooring by material price, installation, subfloor prep, waste, moisture needs and total installed scope.

Updated September 8, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Vinyl plank and laminate are often compared because both can offer a wood-look floor without the same construction as solid hardwood. The better budget comparison includes installation and preparation, not just the box price.

Compare installed cost, not shelf price

A lower material price can be offset by underlayment, trim, floor prep or more difficult installation. Enter the actual material and labor assumptions in the Flooring Cost Calculator.

Check the subfloor first

Flatness, moisture and damage can affect both products. A quote that includes leveling or moisture preparation should not be compared directly with one that assumes a ready-to-install surface.

Plan waste by layout

Room shape and plank layout affect waste. The same waste percentage may not be appropriate for every installation. See flooring waste planning.

Consider wet-area requirements

Product suitability for kitchens, baths, entries or basements depends on the specific flooring system and manufacturer instructions. Do not assume every vinyl or laminate product has the same moisture performance.

Include trim and transitions

Doorways, stairs, reducers, thresholds, baseboards and quarter round can add both material and labor. These details are easy to miss when comparing price per square foot.

Measure before shopping

Calculate the actual rooms with the Square Footage Calculator, then use How Much Flooring Do I Need? to turn area into an order quantity.

Planning note: HomeCostEngine estimates are for budgeting. Verify final measurements, product requirements, permits, code requirements and contractor scope locally before purchasing or hiring.

Frequently asked questions

Is vinyl plank always cheaper than laminate?

No. Product quality, installation method, floor prep, trim and labor can change the total installed cost.

Should I include underlayment in the comparison?

Yes when the selected flooring system requires or benefits from a separate underlayment.

What is the fairest way to compare quotes?

Use the same measured area and compare material, waste, prep, installation, trim and exclusions line by line.
