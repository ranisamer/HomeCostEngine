How Many Cubic Yards Do I Need?

Bulk materials are usually sold by volume, and cubic yards are the standard unit for many U.S. home projects.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

Cubic yards are a volume measurement used for concrete, gravel, soil, mulch and other bulk materials. The calculation is simple when all dimensions use the same units: multiply length × width × depth, then convert cubic feet to cubic yards.

Formula: cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27. For depth in inches, use depth ÷ 12 first.

Worked example: 12 × 16 ft at 4 inches deep

StepCalculationResult
Depth in feet4 ÷ 120.333 ft
Cubic feet12 × 16 × 0.333≈63.94 cu ft
Cubic yards63.94 ÷ 27≈2.37 yd³

That 2.37 yd³ is the geometric volume before any allowance for uneven excavation, spillage, compaction, ordering increments or product-specific factors.

Why the same cubic-yard number can mean different orders

Concrete is often ordered directly by cubic yard. Gravel may be sold by weight. Mulch may be sold by the yard or bag. Soil can settle or compact. So the geometry calculation is the starting point; the purchasing method depends on the material.

When should you add extra material?

Use an allowance only when the project has a reason for it. Examples include irregular excavation, uncertain depth, spillage, compaction, difficult placement or supplier minimums. Avoid adding a generic percentage without considering the job.

Irregular shapes

Break the project into rectangles, triangles or circles, calculate each volume separately and add them together. For sloped or highly irregular excavation, field measurements from a contractor or survey are more dependable than a simple online estimate.

FAQ

Why divide by 27?

One cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet because 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet.

Do I round up?

For ordering, suppliers often have minimum increments. First calculate accurately, then discuss the practical order quantity with the supplier.

Can I use house square footage?

Only if the material footprint actually matches that area. Roofs, patios, beds, driveways and slabs need their own measured dimensions.
