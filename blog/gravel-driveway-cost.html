Gravel Driveway Cost per Square Foot: 2026 Planning Guide

Build a realistic gravel driveway budget by separating stone, delivery, excavation, grading, drainage and compaction instead of relying on material price alone.

Reviewed October 4, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial Team

Gravel driveway cost per square foot is the complete installed project total divided by the measured driveway area. A stone price per ton cannot answer that question on its own. Delivery, soil removal, base repair, grading, drainage and compaction may change the total more than the visible surface material.

Start with the gravel driveway cost calculator to organize quantities and your own local rates. Its price fields are editable planning inputs, not a verified national price survey. This guide explains which scope belongs in those fields and which items need a separate allowance.

Choose the project type before comparing prices

A surface refresh on a sound existing driveway, a repair of recurring ruts and a completely new driveway are different purchases. A refresh may include reshaping and adding surface aggregate. A repair can require removing wet material, restoring drainage and rebuilding isolated areas. A new driveway may need clearing, excavation, subgrade preparation, separation fabric where specified, separate base and surface layers, and an approved road entrance.

Write the project type at the top of every estimate. If a proposal only says “install gravel,” ask for the measured footprint, material specification, layer thicknesses, grading and compaction method. A low total with an undefined structure cannot be compared fairly with a complete installation.

Measure area and calculate each layer separately

For a rectangular section, area equals length times width. Split curves, turnouts and parking pads into measurable sections instead of using one maximum width for the entire drive. The driveway gravel quantity guide covers the takeoff before you attach prices.

Geometric cubic yards = area in square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324. The denominator combines 12 inches per foot and 27 cubic feet per cubic yard. Calculate the base and surface course independently; one depth field should not hide multiple materials.

Be explicit about whether depth describes the finished compacted layer or loose delivered material. The geometric volume does not by itself establish a loose-to-compacted conversion. Ask the installer or supplier how the specified finished thickness translates into an order, then apply only an allowance that is not already included. Use the cubic yard calculator to check individual sections and keep units consistent.

Worked example: a 100-foot driveway

This is an illustrative quantity exercise, not a recommended pavement design. A driveway measuring 100 feet by 12 feet covers 1,200 square feet. If the agreed scope has one 4-inch layer, its geometric volume is 1,200 × 4 ÷ 324 = 14.81 cubic yards.

A documented 10% order allowance would make that 16.30 cubic yards. If, solely for arithmetic, the supplier confirmed 1.5 tons per cubic yard for the applicable order basis, the estimate would be 24.44 tons. That density and allowance are assumptions to replace, not universal rules. Do not treat the 10% as a proven compaction factor.

Now compare a hypothetical two-layer specification: a 6-inch base plus a 2-inch surface course over the same area. Their geometric quantities are 22.22 and 7.41 cubic yards, totaling 29.63 cubic yards before any confirmed conversion or allowance. Each material needs its own density and price. Applying the surface stone price to the full structure would misstate the budget.

Conversely, a 2-inch refresh over an adequate existing structure is only 7.41 geometric cubic yards. The same 1,200-square-foot footprint can therefore represent very different quantities. Soil, drainage and loads determine the actual design; choose those requirements before comparing the dollar-per-square-foot result.

Turn the takeoff into a transparent installed budget

Request current written local pricing with the date, product, sales unit and delivery terms. For each layer, multiply the confirmed order quantity by the supplier's rate. Add load fees, mobilization, preparation, installation, drainage and applicable permit costs without counting an item twice.

Installed planning total = materials + delivery + preparation + placement and compaction + drainage and entrance work + applicable fees.

Comparable cost per square foot = installed planning total ÷ measured area. For the worked driveway, divide the complete total by 1,200. If one contractor includes excavation and another excludes it, add a separately quoted excavation amount to the second proposal before comparing. Do not invent an allowance to make the bids appear equivalent; mark unpriced exclusions as unresolved.

The contractor quote comparison worksheet helps record those differences. Keep owner-supplied material, contractor-supplied material and labor-only proposals clearly labeled. Ask whether tax, equipment hauling, disposal and return visits are included.

Low, typical and high planning cases

  • Low-complexity case: an accessible driveway with a sound base, functioning drainage and a limited surface refresh. Price reshaping, the confirmed surface quantity, delivery and compaction.
  • Typical rebuild case: defined excavation, a specified structure, ordinary access and a clear drainage plan. Budget each layer and the preparation tasks separately.
  • High-complexity case: soft ground, steep grades, restricted truck access, substantial drainage work or a road entrance requiring review. Obtain a site assessment and explicit provisional items before setting a firm budget.

These are scope scenarios, not national dollar ranges or a claim about how often each occurs. Build each total from verified rates and measured quantities. Keep unresolved soil or drainage work visible so the lower scenario is not mistaken for a guaranteed final cost.

Specify the aggregate and drainage work

Base material and driving-surface aggregate serve different functions. The EPA-hosted gravel roads manual, Surface Gravel section explains the importance of grading and the blend of stone, sand and fines. It also distinguishes surface gravel from material intended for other uses. This is road-maintenance background, not a private driveway specification.

Ask the local supplier and installer for an aggregate suitable for the intended layer, rather than substituting decorative gravel because it looks similar. The name “#57 limestone” alone does not establish a complete driveway structure or a reliable tons-per-yard conversion.

Drainage belongs in the initial scope. Inspect where water enters, crosses and leaves the driveway, and ask how grading, ditches or a culvert will handle it. The manual's Drainage section describes why water management affects gravel-road performance. Confirm a lawful outlet instead of directing runoff onto neighboring property.

Regional, permit and maintenance caveats

Quarry distance, available aggregate, haul access, frost conditions, wet soils and local disposal rules can change both the design and cost. Confirm whether work alters a public-road connection, ditch, culvert or right-of-way. For example, MnDOT's permit forms include an access/driveway application for requests involving a trunk highway. That example is not a nationwide permit rule; your road authority and local jurisdiction set the applicable requirements.

Separate the initial installation from future grading, replenishment and drainage maintenance. Request a realistic maintenance plan without assuming a fixed replacement interval. If concrete is an alternative, use the concrete driveway installed-scope comparison over the same footprint, including removal, base and drainage requirements for both options.

What this estimate excludes unless itemized

Utility relocation, retaining walls, major tree removal, contaminated soil, extensive dewatering, engineered drainage, paving at the road entrance and landscaping restoration are not automatically included. Excavation is also not guaranteed by a “site prep” line. Ask for the removal depth, disposal arrangement and change-order trigger. Resolve these items before accepting a fixed total.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate gravel driveway cost per square foot?

Divide the complete installed planning total by the measured driveway area. Include material, delivery, preparation, placement, compaction and specified drainage or entrance work.

How much material does a 100 by 12-foot driveway need?

A single 4-inch layer has a geometric volume of 14.81 cubic yards. Delivered quantity depends on the specified material, loose-to-compacted basis and any separately documented allowance.

Is a gravel refresh comparable to a new driveway?

No. A refresh can reuse an adequate existing structure. A new driveway can require excavation, base construction, drainage and entrance work in addition to the surface layer.

Should I order by tons or cubic yards?

Use the supplier's sales unit and confirm the product-specific conversion on the same loose or compacted basis as your takeoff. Do not assume one density fits every aggregate.

Do gravel driveways require permits?

Requirements depend on the location and work. Check with the local jurisdiction and road authority before changing a road connection, culvert, ditch or right-of-way.
