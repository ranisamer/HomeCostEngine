Roof Tear-Off Cost Per Square in 2026: Layers, Dump Fees and Real Quote Math

Roof removal is often compressed into one line on a proposal even though layer count, material weight, access, disposal and hidden decking can move the price. This guide shows how to separate the math before you compare bids.

Updated September 24, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial • U.S. homeowner planning guide

Quick answer: for asphalt shingles, current national consumer cost data commonly places roof tear-off alone around $1–$3 per square foot, or roughly $100–$300 per roofing square. Heavier materials such as tile or slate can run around $2–$5 per square foot for removal. HomeGuide's current 2026 guide publishes those broad ranges and also notes that multiple layers and dumpster costs can increase the total. See the source data from HomeGuide.

Run your own numbers: open the Roof Tear-Off Cost Calculator, enter actual roof surface area, existing material, layer count and a separate disposal allowance, then keep the result beside each contractor proposal.

What “per square” means in a tear-off quote

A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface. If a contractor quotes $175 per square for removal, a 20-square roof starts with a $3,500 removal line before any separate dump fee or special-access surcharge. The important phrase is “roof surface.” A 2,000-square-foot house is not necessarily a 20-square roof because pitch, overhangs, attached garages, porches and story count change roof area.

If your measurements are still uncertain, use the Roof Pitch Calculator and Roofing Square Calculator before comparing tear-off prices. That prevents a common mistake: negotiating a unit price while the bidders are using different roof quantities.

These are screening ranges, not a bid. They do not prove that your project should land at the midpoint, and they should not be added blindly to a quote that already includes disposal and labor.

Why a second roof layer changes the price

Multiple layers are not simply “a little more material.” A second layer can mean more fasteners, more loading cycles, more debris weight and more disposal volume. If one roofer assumes one layer and another has already priced two, the lower total may not actually be the lower scope.

Before signing, ask for one sentence in the proposal that says how many existing layers are included and how an additional layer will be priced if discovered. This matters because the real layer count may not be obvious from the ground.

Keep disposal visible instead of burying it

HomeGuide's 2026 tear-off guide separately references dumpster rental as a cost factor. That is useful because contractors structure removal differently: one bid may include debris hauling inside a per-square number while another may add a container or disposal line later. Your comparison sheet should have separate columns for removal labor and disposal unless every bidder confirms they are bundled the same way.

The Contractor Quote Comparison tool is designed for exactly this problem. A “cheaper” bid can stop being cheaper once omitted layers, disposal or decking allowances are normalized.

Example: 1,500 sq ft roof with one asphalt layer

Suppose the actual roof surface is 1,500 sq ft, or 15 roofing squares. Using the broad $1–$3 removal range gives a screening allowance of $1,500–$4,500. If the contractor separately charges $450 for a dumpster, the planning range becomes $1,950–$4,950. That does not mean a local bid outside this range is wrong; it tells you which assumptions to ask about.

Example: 2,500 sq ft roof with two layers

A 2,500 sq ft roof is 25 squares. The national one-layer benchmark is only a starting point. A second layer may increase labor and dump weight materially. Instead of inventing a universal “two-layer price,” enter the second layer in the tear-off calculator, then replace the calculator's planning adjustment with the roofer's written extra-layer price when you receive bids.

Tear-off and decking are different line items

Removal exposes the roof deck; it does not tell you how much decking must be replaced. Concealed sheathing damage should be handled with a separate quantity and unit rate. Use the Roof Decking Replacement Cost Calculator and ask for an installed per-sheet rate before work starts.

This separation matters because otherwise a contractor can present one large “wood and tear-off” allowance that is hard to verify after the roof is open.

Safety is part of the labor you are buying

Roof removal involves fall exposure, debris handling and weather protection. OSHA maintains specific residential construction fall-protection guidance covering roofing and weatherproofing work. A homeowner comparing quotes should not try to force a contractor to remove legitimate safety setup just to reduce the line item.

What a strong tear-off scope should say

  • Measured roof area or roofing-square quantity used for pricing.
  • Existing material and number of layers included.
  • Whether underlayment removal is included.
  • Whether dumpster, hauling and disposal are included or separate.
  • Site protection for landscaping, windows, driveways and pools.
  • What happens if extra layers are discovered.
  • How damaged decking will be documented, approved and priced.
  • Whether steep-slope, multi-story or limited-access charges are already included.

How this connects to the full roof budget

Tear-off is only one part of replacement. A complete plan can also include underlayment, finished roofing, starter and ridge materials, flashing, ventilation, permits, decking and contingency. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to tear off shingles per roofing square?

Current 2026 national consumer guides commonly place asphalt-shingle removal around $100 to $300 per roofing square, before project-specific access, layer count and disposal differences.

Is a second layer included in roof tear-off cost?

Not automatically. Proposals should state how many layers are included and how additional layers will be priced if discovered.

Does roof tear-off include a dumpster?

It may or may not. Some contractors bundle disposal into the removal rate while others list dumpsters, hauling or dump fees separately.

Should I calculate tear-off from house square footage?

No. Use actual roof surface area or roofing squares because pitch, overhangs, garages and attached structures can make roof area different from living area.

When is damaged decking priced?

Decking condition is often confirmed after tear-off. Get a written installed per-sheet or per-square-foot rate before work begins so concealed damage does not become an undefined change order.

Planning note: National ranges are useful for screening a budget, not for approving work. Replace assumptions with measured quantities, product specifications, local requirements and itemized contractor pricing.
