How Much Paint Do I Need for a Room?

A good paint estimate starts with wall area, then adjusts for openings, number of coats and real product coverage.

Reviewed October 1, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial Team

Quick answer: calculate the area of the walls you will paint, subtract measured unpainted openings, multiply by finish coats, and divide by the selected paint's coverage per gallon. A hypothetical 12 × 14 ft room with 8 ft walls, one 3 × 7 ft door and two 3 × 5 ft windows has 365 sq ft of paintable wall area. Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon require 2.09 gallons before any reserve or package rounding.

Use the room Paint Calculator for an initial estimate, then compare its standard opening allowances with your actual measurements. A room's floor area does not tell you its wall area. Keeping those quantities separate helps you buy enough coating without treating every bedroom as a fixed “two-gallon job.”

The room paint formula

For a rectangular room with walls of equal height, the perimeter is twice the length plus twice the width. Multiply that perimeter by wall height to get gross wall area. Then subtract doors, windows and other surfaces that will not receive the wall paint.

Net wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − unpainted opening area.
Finish paint gallons = net wall area × finish coats ÷ coverage per gallon.

All lengths in this formula are in feet, areas are in square feet, and coverage is square feet per U.S. gallon for one coat. Calculate separate wall sections for an irregular room, alcove or sloped ceiling rather than forcing them into a rectangle. Our Square Footage Calculator can help total rectangular sections. Do not count a closet unless it is included in the painting scope.

Worked example: a 12 × 14 ft room

This example uses measured openings and hypothetical coverage, not a guarantee for a particular paint. The room has 8 ft walls, a 3 × 7 ft door and two 3 × 5 ft windows. Only the four walls are being painted; the ceiling, door faces and trim are separate.

  1. Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 14) = 52 ft.
  2. Gross wall area: 52 × 8 = 416 sq ft.
  3. Opening area: (3 × 7) + 2 × (3 × 5) = 51 sq ft.
  4. Net wall area: 416 − 51 = 365 sq ft.
  5. Two-coat coverage demand: 365 × 2 = 730 sq ft.
  6. Unrounded finish paint: 730 ÷ 350 = 2.09 gallons.

A 10% planning reserve would make the estimate (730 ÷ 350) × 1.10 = about 2.29 gallons. That percentage is an illustrative choice for minor measurement and application losses, not a manufacturer requirement. If the product is sold in matching one-gallon and quart containers, two gallons plus two quarts provide 2.50 gallons. Three one-gallon cans provide more reserve. Check actual container sizes, availability and tinting policies before choosing the shopping quantity.

Keep the unrounded result visible. Rounding 2.09 to two gallons leaves less paint than the calculation calls for; rounding every small wall section separately can cause excessive purchasing. Total surfaces using the same product, color and finish first, then choose the available package combination.

Low, typical and high quantity planning

Coverage is the most important product input after area and coats. Behr's interior quantity guidance gives a 250–400 sq ft one-coat coverage range for the specific interior products it identifies. It also explains that texture, porosity and substantial color changes can increase requirements. That range is not a specification for every coating.

The table holds our 365 sq ft room and two finish coats constant so you can see the effect of coverage alone. “Typical” is a middle planning assumption here, not a measured national average. Use only a coverage value supported by your selected product and surface.

Do not assume one finish coat simply because the container says “paint and primer.” Follow the exact product's preparation and application instructions. If three finish coats are required at 350 sq ft per gallon, the same room needs 365 × 3 ÷ 350 = 3.13 gallons before reserve. Count primer separately so its coverage is not confused with finish-paint coverage.

Ceilings, primer, doors and trim need separate lines

The flat ceiling in our example is 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft. At a hypothetical 350 sq ft per gallon and two coats, it needs 168 × 2 ÷ 350 = 0.96 gallons before reserve. If ceiling paint differs from wall paint, that quantity cannot be covered by the wall-paint purchase. A sloped ceiling needs its actual surface area.

For primer, list which surfaces need it and its own coverage. If the entire 365 sq ft wall area requires one primer coat at a hypothetical 300 sq ft per gallon, the primer quantity is 1.22 gallons. Spot priming repaired areas may have a different quantity; confirm whether the product instructions call for spot treatment or a full coat.

Door faces, baseboards, casing and crown molding may use another coating or sheen. Measure their painted surfaces separately, including which door faces are in scope. Subtracting a doorway from wall area does not calculate paint for the door itself. For several rooms, the whole-house paint takeoff guide explains why room-by-room surface totals are more useful than a floor-area shortcut.

What the quantity includes and excludes

This method estimates liquid coating for the specified surfaces and coat counts. It does not include patching compound, caulk, masking materials, roller covers, brushes, drop cloths or labor. It also does not estimate the cost of moving furniture, repairing drywall, removing wallpaper, correcting moisture damage or working in tall stairwells.

For the full project budget, use the cost-to-paint-a-room guide after the takeoff. Multiply the shopping quantity by a verified local price for the actual product, then price preparation and other supplies separately. Do not treat the gallons result as an installed contractor quote.

Preparation, regional conditions and permits

Surface condition matters more to quantity than your ZIP code. Rough texture, porous repairs and changing colors can affect coverage. Local product availability, environmental restrictions and disposal rules can affect the purchase and cleanup plan. Follow the label for temperature, ventilation, drying and recoat conditions; a deadline does not justify shortening the specified recoat interval.

In an older home, preparation can also raise a safety issue. EPA's consumer guidance for pre-1978 homes recommends hiring a lead-safe certified contractor when renovation or painting work may disturb lead-based paint. Review that guidance before sanding or scraping existing coatings. Lead-safe scope is separate from the gallons calculation.

Check with the local building department if the project includes wall alterations, significant repairs or other work beyond a cosmetic repaint, and confirm any property or association requirements. This guide does not establish a permit exemption. When hiring, ask bidders to state surfaces, preparation, primer, finish coats, product line, protection and cleanup. The Contractor Quote Comparison Tool helps compare those written inclusions.

A useful buying checklist

  • Measure each wall and actual openings; confirm that deducted areas will remain unpainted.
  • Separate products by surface, color and sheen.
  • Use the current label's coverage and coat instructions.
  • Calculate primer independently from finish paint.
  • Add a documented reserve, then round once to available packages.
  • Label any retained touch-up paint with room, color, product and purchase date, and follow storage instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Is one gallon enough for a room?

It depends on paintable area, coats and coverage. Our example needs 2.09 gallons for two coats of the walls at 350 sq ft per gallon, before reserve.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Subtract their measured area when they will not receive the wall paint. Keep any allowance for application loss separate, and calculate painted door faces and trim independently.

Does the Paint Calculator include ceilings?

The room calculator estimates four walls using room dimensions and standard opening allowances. Calculate ceiling area separately and verify actual opening dimensions for the final purchase.

How much extra paint should I buy?

Choose a documented reserve based on surface uncertainty, application loss and touch-up needs, then check package sizes. The 10% reserve in this example is illustrative, not a universal requirement.

Can I use floor square footage to estimate paint?

Floor area alone misses wall height, room perimeter, openings and coat count. Measure the surfaces receiving each product for a usable quantity.
