Heat Pump Installation Cost: What Changes the Price?

Heat pump pricing is not just the outdoor unit. The final project can include indoor equipment, controls, duct modifications, electrical work and commissioning.

Updated September 21, 2026 • HomeCostEngine Editorial

A heat pump quote is a package of equipment and installation work. The outdoor unit is only one line. Indoor equipment, controls, electrical work, refrigerant piping, condensate, duct changes and commissioning can all move the project total.

Start with the system configuration

ConfigurationWhat usually changes in the scope
Ducted heat pumpIndoor air handler or coil, duct compatibility, airflow and controls
Ductless mini-splitNumber/location of heads, line-set routing, condensate and electrical feeds
Dual-fuel / hybridHeat pump plus combustion equipment, controls and changeover logic
System conversionPotential duct, electrical, thermostat and equipment-location changes

Capacity should come from the building, not a square-foot shortcut

Ask how the contractor selected the equipment size. Climate, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, internal loads and duct performance all influence the home's load. Oversizing can create comfort and humidity problems just as undersizing can create capacity problems.

Look at low-temperature performance if winters are cold

For cold-climate projects, ask for capacity information at relevant outdoor temperatures and how backup heat is expected to operate. Comparing SEER2 alone does not answer a winter heating question.

Ductwork can turn a replacement into a larger project

A new heat pump still depends on the air distribution system. High static pressure, undersized returns, leakage or poorly balanced branches can limit performance. A proposal that includes duct correction is not directly comparable with one that assumes every existing duct is fine.

Electrical scope is easy to miss

Panel capacity, breakers, disconnects, conductor size, auxiliary heat and equipment location can all affect electrical work. Ask whether the quoted price assumes the existing service can support the new system.

Commissioning is part of the installation

Startup should include more than “it turns on.” Ask what the contractor documents for airflow, static pressure, refrigerant charge or manufacturer startup requirements, thermostat setup and drainage. The exact procedure depends on the system.

Compare bids with the same equipment information

  • Outdoor model number
  • Indoor model number
  • Matched-system efficiency ratings
  • Heating performance / backup strategy
  • Duct modifications
  • Electrical work
  • Controls
  • Permit and commissioning

Use the calculator for scenarios, not sizing

The HVAC Replacement Cost Calculator is useful for comparing budget assumptions. Final sizing and equipment selection should come from the actual home and local design conditions.

Sources

  • ENERGY STAR — Air-Source Heat Pumps
When two quotes differ: compare equipment combinations, duct scope, electrical scope and commissioning before assuming the price difference is only contractor markup.
