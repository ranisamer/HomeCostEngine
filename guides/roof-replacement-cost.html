Roofing cost guide

Roof replacement cost: what actually changes the price?

A useful roof budget starts with the roof—not just the home’s floor area. Material, geometry, tear-off, access and scope can move two quotes thousands of dollars apart.

Calculate Your Roof Cost →

Start with roof area

Contractors price roofing against roof surface area, commonly expressed in square feet or roofing squares. One roofing square equals 100 square feet. A two-story 2,000-square-foot home does not automatically have a 2,000-square-foot roof.

Material changes both price and lifespan

Asphalt shingles are commonly selected for lower upfront cost. Metal, tile, wood and slate can cost more to install and may also require different underlayment, flashing, fasteners or structural considerations.

Complex roofs cost more to work on

Steep slopes, multiple valleys, dormers, skylights and limited access increase labor time and safety requirements. Tear-off and disposal also matter, especially when multiple layers or damaged decking are discovered.

Compare scope before price

Ask contractors to separate tear-off, decking allowance, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, materials, labor, permits, cleanup and warranty terms. A quote that looks cheaper can simply include less.

Use a range, not a fake “exact” number

Online tools are strongest as budgeting aids. Your local contractor still needs to inspect the roof and confirm measurements, code requirements and hidden conditions.
