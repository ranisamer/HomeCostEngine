Kitchen Remodel Cost: Build the Scope Before the Budget

The same-size kitchen can produce very different bids. Cabinets, layout changes, appliance allowances, plumbing and electrical work, finish level and local labor matter more than square footage alone.

Reviewed September 21, 2026 • U.S. planning guide

Price the decisions first

Kitchen budgets usually become unreliable when too many decisions are still hidden inside allowances. Before comparing totals, decide what is staying, what is moving and which selections are still open. A contractor can price a clear scope; a vague “new kitchen” leaves too much room for assumptions.

Three very different kinds of kitchen project

Project typeUsually staysUsually changes
RefreshLayout, most utilities, many cabinetsPaint, hardware, lighting, backsplash, selected finishes
Full replacementBasic room footprintCabinets, counters, appliances, finishes, some electrical/plumbing
Layout changeVery little by assumptionWalls, utilities, ventilation, cabinetry plan and sometimes structure

Cabinets set more than the cabinet budget

Cabinet decisions affect countertops, appliance openings, lighting, flooring transitions and the installation sequence. Ask whether finished panels, fillers, crown, toe-kick, hardware, delivery and installation are included. A cabinet quote that excludes those pieces can look cheaper than it really is.

Appliances can change construction scope

A new range may need a different circuit or gas connection. A larger refrigerator can change cabinet dimensions. A hood can require new duct routing. Keep the appliance purchase price separate from the construction work the appliance creates.

Do a “move / keep / replace” pass before requesting bids

  • Keep: walls, sink location, existing floor, service panel, windows?
  • Move: island, sink, range, doorway, lighting?
  • Replace: cabinets, counters, appliances, backsplash, fixtures?

This ten-minute exercise often explains more about the eventual budget than the room's square footage.

Contingency should cover unknowns, not missing decisions

If you have not selected countertops yet, use an allowance. If opening a wall might reveal outdated wiring or hidden damage, that is contingency territory. The Project Contingency Calculator keeps those two buckets separate.

Before signing

  1. Attach the cabinet plan and finish schedule to the proposal.
  2. List model numbers for owner-supplied appliances when known.
  3. Identify who obtains permits and closes inspections.
  4. Define demolition, protection and daily cleanup.
  5. Write down exclusions instead of relying on assumptions.
  6. Require written change orders before extra work proceeds.
