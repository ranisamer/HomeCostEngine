Bathroom Remodel Cost: Price the Hidden Work, Not Just the Tile

Bathrooms pack plumbing, waterproofing, electrical, ventilation and finish work into a small space. A useful budget needs to account for what is behind the walls as well as what you can see.

Choose the remodel level first

Surface refresh

Keep the layout and most plumbing in place. Work may include paint, fixtures, vanity, mirror, lighting, hardware, selected tile or flooring and minor repairs.

Standard full remodel

Replace the vanity, flooring, shower or tub finishes, plumbing fixtures, lighting and ventilation while keeping major fixture locations largely unchanged. Waterproofing and substrate work become important quote items.

Layout-changing / premium remodel

Move plumbing, alter walls, build a custom shower, add premium tile or glass, expand the room or change electrical and ventilation substantially. Coordination between trades and inspections can drive both cost and schedule.

The cost drivers homeowners often miss

Waterproofing: ask for the exact shower/tub waterproofing system and what substrate preparation is included. Tile is a finish; it is not the waterproofing layer.

Plumbing relocation: keeping a toilet, shower and vanity near existing rough-ins usually reduces complexity compared with moving drains or supply lines.

Tile labor: tile size, pattern, niches, benches, curbs, transitions and wall coverage can change installation time significantly.

Ventilation: fan sizing, duct routing and exterior termination can add work, especially in older homes.

Hidden damage: leaks, rot, mold-related remediation, subfloor damage and previous nonstandard work may not be visible until demolition.

Bathroom quote checklist

Compare the same scope: one proposal may include waterproofing, glass, haul-away and permit work while another leaves them as allowances or exclusions. Ask contractors to identify these items explicitly.
  1. Demolition, protection and debris disposal.
  2. Subfloor and wall preparation assumptions.
  3. Waterproofing system and installation scope.
  4. Tile quantity, pattern, grout and trim pieces.
  5. Vanity, top, sink, faucet and fixture allowances.
  6. Shower glass, tub or shower base specification.
  7. Plumbing relocation or fixture replacement.
  8. Electrical, lighting, GFCI and ventilation work.
  9. Permit and inspection responsibility.
  10. Paint, trim, accessories, cleanup and warranty.

Plan contingency separately

A bathroom in an older home or a project that opens wet walls and floors may expose conditions that cannot be priced confidently before demolition. The Bathroom Remodel Cost Calculator lets you see the reserve separately from the known project scope.

Permit and code questions

Requirements vary by location. Plumbing relocation, electrical changes, ventilation, structural work and some full remodels may require permits and inspections. Confirm the local rules before signing a contract and identify who is responsible for scheduling inspections and corrections.

Use the estimate to improve your bids

After building a planning range, request multiple itemized bids based on the same fixture list and finish scope. Use the Contractor Quote Comparison to compare allowances, exclusions and warranty details, not just total price.
