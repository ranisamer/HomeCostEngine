Bathroom Remodel Cost: Price the Hidden Work, Not Just the Tile

Bathrooms pack plumbing, waterproofing, electrical, ventilation and finish work into a small space. A useful budget needs to account for what is behind the walls as well as what you can see.

Reviewed September 21, 2026 • U.S. planning guide

Bathrooms are small rooms with a lot of hidden scope

A bathroom can look simple in a photo and still involve plumbing, waterproofing, ventilation, electrical work, tile setting, glass, cabinetry and several inspection points. The finish selections matter, but the parts behind the tile are often where scope differences begin.

Decide how far the demolition goes

ApproachTypical goalQuestions to answer
RefreshImprove finishes with limited opening of wallsWhat can stay? Are fixtures and waterproofed areas being disturbed?
Full remodelReplace most finishes and fixturesWhat substrates, waterproofing and rough-ins are included?
Layout changeMove fixtures or enlarge the roomWhere do drains, supplies, walls and ventilation need to move?

Ask about the waterproofing system by name

“Tile shower included” is not enough detail. Ask what backer or substrate is used, what waterproofing system is installed, how corners and penetrations are treated and whether the contractor follows the selected system's installation requirements.

Moving the toilet is not the same as changing the faucet

Fixture relocation can require changes to drains, venting, framing or floor structure. Before assuming a layout move is simple, ask the plumber or contractor what has to happen below and behind the finished surfaces.

Tile scope needs more detail than square footage

  • Tile dimensions and layout
  • Wall height and number of tiled walls
  • Niches, benches and curbs
  • Edge/trim treatment
  • Grout type and joint width
  • Substrate flattening or repair

What to do with hidden-damage risk

Water staining, soft subfloor, previous leaks and older remodels can create unknown conditions. Do not bury those risks inside a finish allowance. Keep a separate owner contingency and require documentation before concealed-condition work is added.

Normalize bids before comparing price

ItemWhat should be clear
DemolitionHow far surfaces are removed and who hauls debris
WaterproofingNamed system and installation scope
PlumbingFixture replacement vs relocation
ElectricalLighting, receptacles, fan and circuit assumptions
GlassIncluded, allowance or owner-supplied
FinishesTile, vanity, top, fixtures, paint and trim

Before work begins

  1. Confirm selections and allowances in writing.
  2. Clarify permit and inspection responsibility.
  3. Document the condition of adjacent rooms.
  4. Agree on how concealed damage is photographed and approved.
  5. Keep change orders written and priced before extra work proceeds.

One useful test: if two bathroom quotes differ sharply, compare waterproofing, substrate preparation, glass, plumbing relocation and finish allowances before assuming one contractor is simply more expensive.
