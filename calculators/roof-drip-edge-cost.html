Roof drip edge cost per linear foot

Drip edge is usually sold and estimated in linear feet. A broad 2026 replacement benchmark of $1.50–$9 per linear foot can help screen a budget, but a small repair and a full reroof detail are not the same job.

How much drip edge do I need?

Add the eave and rake lengths included in the project, then add a modest allowance for cuts and overlaps. Divide by the stock piece length and round up. For example, 200 measured feet with a 10% allowance becomes 220 planned feet, or 22 ten-foot pieces.

Material quantity and installed cost are different

The number of pieces answers what to purchase. The installed rate can also include removal, fastening, corners, access, fascia condition and integration with underlayment and roofing. Keep the two numbers separate when comparing quotes.

What to ask the roofer

  • Which eaves and rakes receive new drip edge.
  • Metal type, finish and stock length.
  • Whether old metal removal is included.
  • Whether damaged fascia or sheathing is excluded.
  • How the drip edge integrates with underlayment and the finished roof system.

Sources and methodology

The broad replacement benchmark is based on HomeGuide's 2026 roof repair cost guide. Quantity math is transparent: measured length × allowance, then rounded to whole stock pieces.

Planning limit: Verify project-specific installation details, product requirements and locally adopted code with a qualified roofing professional.

Frequently asked questions

How much does roof drip edge cost per linear foot?

Published 2026 consumer cost guides show a broad replacement range around $1.50–$9 per linear foot. Actual quotes can fall outside that range.

How many 10-foot pieces do I need?

Divide planned length after allowance by 10 and round up. The calculator does this automatically.

Should I add waste?

A small allowance can cover cuts and overlaps, but use the project layout and product lengths rather than adding arbitrary waste.

Is drip edge included in a roof replacement?

It may be, but ask the contractor to identify new versus reused edge metal and whether removal and fascia repair are included.
