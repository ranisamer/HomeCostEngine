What the calculator is doing

It turns three dimensions into volume. Length and width stay in feet, depth is converted from inches to feet, and the resulting cubic feet are divided by 27 because one cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet.

Worked example: 20 × 10 ft at 4 inches deep

Four inches is 0.333 ft. Multiply 20 × 10 × 0.333 to get about 66.7 cubic feet. Divide by 27 and the base volume is about 2.47 cubic yards. A 5% allowance would bring that to roughly 2.59 yd³.

Why the material matters after the math

The geometry tells you how much space needs to be filled. It does not tell you how a supplier will sell the product. Ready-mix is commonly ordered by cubic yard. Gravel may be sold by the ton. Mulch may be sold by the yard or by bag. Topsoil can settle. The volume calculation stays the same; the purchasing step changes.

Do not hide irregular sections inside “waste”

If the project has a thickened edge, footing, step or deeper low spot, calculate that section separately and add it to the base volume. A waste allowance should cover uncertainty and handling loss, not missing geometry.
