Built for how U.S. homeowners actually budget

More than a single national average

A national average can be useful for orientation, but it rarely describes one specific house. This calculator exposes the assumptions that often get hidden: project size, finish level, local market pressure, home age, access complexity, removal work, permits and contingency.

What is included in the estimate?

The base range is an installed-cost planning model. The result then separates an approximate materials share and labor share, while showing demolition/disposal, a permit allowance and your selected contingency as separate planning items. Exact trade splits vary by project and contractor.

Why home age matters

Older homes can add uncertainty through outdated wiring or plumbing, hidden damage, nonstandard framing, lead/asbestos procedures, code upgrades and previous alterations. The age adjustment is a sensitivity control, not a diagnosis of your property.

How to use this with contractor bids

  1. Run the calculator with the same scope you plan to request.
  2. Ask for itemized bids with allowances and exclusions shown.
  3. Compare at least three proposals using the same assumptions.
  4. Check licensing, insurance, warranty, payment schedule and permit responsibility.
  5. Use the Contractor Quote Comparison tool before choosing.

Frequently asked questions

Home renovation budgeting FAQ

How accurate is a home renovation cost calculator?

It is most useful for early planning and quote sense-checking. Site conditions, local wages, material selections, contractor overhead, permits and concealed work can move the final price.

Should I budget extra for surprises?

Often, yes. A contingency is especially useful when the home is older, walls or floors will be opened, or the scope is not fully defined. Keep contingency separate from known work so you can see what you are actually reserving for uncertainty.

Does the cheapest quote usually save money?

Not necessarily. Compare scope, allowances, exclusions, product specifications, disposal, permits, warranty and change-order rules—not only the total price.
