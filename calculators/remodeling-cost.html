Why this is more useful than one national average

Home improvement pricing changes across the U.S., but a ZIP code alone does not magically reveal a contractor's actual bid. HomeCostEngine uses the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis 2024 Regional Price Parity for the selected state as a broad price-level baseline, then deliberately applies only 65% of that deviation from the U.S. average. This prevents a general consumer price index from being treated as a construction-specific price quote.

The optional within-state setting then lets you test a lower-cost or higher-cost market. It remains a sensitivity control: actual construction labor, materials, permits, disposal, climate requirements and contractor availability can differ substantially inside one state.

What is included in the estimate?

The base range is an installed-cost planning model. The result separates an approximate materials share and labor share, while showing demolition/disposal, a permit allowance and your selected contingency as separate planning items. Exact trade splits vary by project and contractor.

How to use this with contractor bids

  1. Select the project and your state.
  2. Match the finish level and scope as closely as possible.
  3. Ask contractors for itemized bids with allowances and exclusions shown.
  4. Compare at least three proposals using the same assumptions.
  5. Use the Contractor Quote Comparison tool before choosing.

Data source

The state baseline uses 2024 Regional Price Parities published by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis. The 2024 state RPP release was published February 19, 2026. HomeCostEngine stores the published values locally so the calculator works instantly without sending your inputs to an external pricing service.

Frequently asked questions

Home renovation budgeting FAQ

How does the state adjustment work?

The U.S. baseline is 100. A state above 100 has a higher general price level and a state below 100 has a lower one. HomeCostEngine applies only 65% of that difference to the project model, then lets you adjust for a lower- or higher-cost area within the state.

Is this ZIP-code contractor pricing?

No. We do not label a generalized multiplier as an exact ZIP price. A real quote still depends on trade labor, permit jurisdiction, access, product choices, schedule and contractor scope.

Should I budget extra for surprises?

Often, yes. A contingency is especially useful when the home is older, walls or floors will be opened, or the scope is not fully defined. Keep contingency separate from known work so you can see what you are reserving for uncertainty.
