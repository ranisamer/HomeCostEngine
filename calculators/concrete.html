Check the math yourself

From a slab sketch to an auditable order

Rectangle volume in yd³ = length in ft × width in ft × thickness in inches ÷ 324. Sum the sections, add known extra geometry, apply your chosen allowance, then round up to your supplier's entered increment. For the full ordering discussion, use the guide to extra concrete, thickened edges and order allowances.

Worked example: 20 × 12 ft at 4 inches

The slab contains exactly 80 ft³, or 2.963 yd³. A chosen 10% allowance adds 8 ft³, bringing the planning quantity to 3.259 yd³. If the supplier confirms 0.25-yd³ increments, rounding up produces 3.5 yd³. That last 0.241 yd³ comes from rounding, not from slab geometry.

At a hypothetical $160/yd³ plus a hypothetical $75 delivery charge, material plus that charge is 3.5 × $160 + $75 = $635. These are arithmetic inputs, not current price estimates. At the example yield of 0.60 ft³ per bag, the unrounded 88 ft³ requires 147 bags; ready-mix increment rounding is not applied to the bag alternative.

How the thickened edge avoids double counting

For the same slab, suppose the approved perimeter is 12 inches wide and 12 inches deep in total. The inside rectangle is 18 × 10 ft, so the edge ring covers 240 − 180 = 60 sq ft. Only 8 inches below the 4-inch slab is additional: 60 × 8 ÷ 12 = 40 ft³ extra. The total before allowance becomes 120 ft³, or 4.444 yd³. Do not add the entire edge depth again or count overlapping corner rectangles.

This option assumes a uniform ring inside Section 1, with vertical sides and constant depth. Sloped edges, steps, isolated piers and irregular excavations need their own takeoff. Use the cubic-yard calculator for other separate volumes. Structural dimensions, reinforcement, mix specification and code compliance come from your approved design and local professionals.

Compare the complete supply and installation scope

A per-yard rate does not automatically cover delivery, short loads, pumping, waiting time, washout or tax. Read what belongs in a concrete supplier quote, then use the contractor quote comparison to separate material supply from excavation, base work, forms, reinforcement and finishing.

Bag counts describe material quantity, not the crew, mixer capacity or placement time required to complete a continuous pour. The ready-mix versus bags guide explains that practical decision.
