Start with geometry, then think about the pour

The calculator converts length × width × thickness into cubic feet, divides by 27 to get cubic yards, then applies the allowance you select. That gives a quantity target. It does not yet tell you the final delivered order or installed project cost.

Worked example: 20 × 12 ft slab at 4 inches

Four inches is 0.333 ft. The theoretical volume is 20 × 12 × 0.333 = about 79.9 cubic feet. Divide by 27 and the slab is about 2.96 yd³ before an allowance. With 5% extra, the planning quantity is about 3.11 yd³.

Do not use “waste” to hide missing geometry

If the slab has a thickened edge, footing, step or deeper section, calculate that volume separately and add it. A percentage allowance is for uncertainty and handling loss, not for parts of the project you already know exist.

What usually sits outside the per-yard material price

ItemWhy it matters
Delivery / short loadSmall orders can carry fixed charges or minimums.
Pumping or buggy placementNeeded when the truck cannot discharge close to the forms.
Base preparationExcavation, aggregate and compaction are separate from concrete volume.
ReinforcementRebar, mesh or fibers depend on the project design.
Forms and finishingLabor can be a large part of installed cost.
Washout / cleanupSite logistics should be planned before delivery.

Three checks before ordering

  1. Re-measure the forms after they are set.
  2. Confirm the designed thickness and any thickened sections.
  3. Call the ready-mix supplier for minimum order, increment, delivery and waiting-time rules.

Allowance scenarios

Use the selector to test 5%, 10% and 15% rather than assuming one percentage is always correct. An accurately formed slab over a well-prepared base can have less uncertainty than an irregular excavation with changing depth.
