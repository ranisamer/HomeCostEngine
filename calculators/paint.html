The room size is not the paintable area

The calculator estimates the four walls from room length, width and wall height. It then subtracts a simple door/window allowance, multiplies by coats and divides by the coverage figure you enter.

Worked example: 14 × 12 ft room, 8 ft walls

The room perimeter is 52 ft. Multiply by 8 ft and gross wall area is 416 sq ft. If one door and two windows remove about 51 sq ft, net wall area is roughly 365 sq ft. Two coats create about 730 sq ft of coverage demand.

Coverage is a product input, not a universal constant

The default 350 sq ft per gallon is only a starting assumption. Use the coverage shown on the product's current label or technical data. Rough masonry, new drywall, porous surfaces and major color changes can use more coating than a smooth previously painted wall.

Primer belongs on its own line

Primer may be appropriate for new drywall, stains, repaired areas, glossy surfaces or difficult color changes. If primer is required, calculate it separately rather than treating it as another finish coat.

Ceilings, trim and doors are separate scopes

What the calculator does not price

It estimates paint material only. Professional painting quotes may include patching, sanding, caulking, masking, furniture moving, ceiling work, trim, doors, primer, cleanup and multiple mobilizations.

Older-home safety

If the project disturbs painted surfaces in a pre-1978 home, review applicable lead-safe requirements before sanding, scraping or demolition. The U.S. EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting program is the relevant federal starting point.

How to use it

Measure surfaces before pricing the room

  1. Enter the room dimensions and openings. Floor area alone does not tell you paintable wall area.
  2. Use the product's real coverage. Coverage, coats and primer needs should come from the paint you are actually buying.
  3. Keep other surfaces separate. Ceilings, trim, doors and repair work should not be hidden inside the wall-paint result.
