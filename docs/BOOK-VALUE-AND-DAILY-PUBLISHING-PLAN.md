# HomeCostEngine: value, pricing and daily publishing plan

Prepared 2026-10-25. Audience: U.S. homeowners making a specific purchase or project decision. Current store currency: GBP. Publication is not evidence of buyer demand. No sales, rankings, savings, conversion rates or reader outcomes have been validated by this review.

## What was reviewed and what is still unknown

Reviewed the 20-product manifest, current product-page structure, manuscript architecture and detailed energy-guide content, checkout/delivery integration tests and existing funnel instrumentation. This is a commercial/editorial assessment, not a 20-person reader trial or a market-pricing study. No live Stripe revenue export, current U.S. acquisition report, refunds dataset or customer interviews were available for this assessment. Existing technical tests prove entitlement and delivery behavior, not willingness to pay.

Every current book has 64 pages, 19 worked cases and 19 fillable worksheets. Most topic books adapt six general owner-budget chapters and contain thirteen topic chapters. That overlap is disclosed; it weakens the reason to buy several similar titles. Books 11 and 12 have independently authored sequences but still share some ownership concepts with books 1 and 9. Do not recommend every overlapping book to the same buyer.

Free official resources already cover many facts. Paid value must be the organized decision workflow: a completed example, reusable records, comparison method, traceable arithmetic and a concrete next action. Page count, AI production speed and links to free sources do not by themselves justify a paid product.

## Commercial value gate for each new book

1. Name one reader and one immediate decision. Research intent with current sources and the site's U.S. Search Console queries when available. Never invent search volumes or claim an unobserved query is high demand.
2. Check existing books, blog posts and the queue. At least 80% of substantive new-book content must be original to that title under this internal editorial rule; track reused methods. Do not publish a new title that is mainly a rewrite of an existing one.
3. Supply at least three complete, independently checked worked cases, five usable decision templates or worksheets, a copy-ready provider-question list and an outcome the reader can complete in one sitting. Templates are planning aids, not legal contracts or professional designs.
4. Let useful content determine length. A focused 24–40-page workbook can be better than padding to 64 pages. State actual pages, formats and fields everywhere.
5. Verify relevant primary sources, link each factual claim near its support, record access dates and time-sensitive restrictions, and distinguish original hypothetical examples from sourced facts. Do not reproduce official guidance as a paid compilation.
6. Provide actual cover and interior previews, who it helps, who should skip it, what's additional to the free post, limitations and overlap. Never add fabricated reviews, urgency, savings guarantees or misleading list prices.
7. Build and visually check the PDF; check formulas, fields and internal links. Validate the exact GBP price, checkout, paid entitlement, private download, thank-you title/link, product schema and responsive page. No live charge or customer email is authorized just for testing.
8. Publish one distinct companion article with a free useful result, a worked example and 2–4 relevant verified primary references. Add a contextual book link, blog-index link, reciprocal product link and sitemap entry. Do not copy the article body across topics.

If a gate fails, keep that day's work as a draft and report the reason. A daily target must not force a duplicate or unverified publication.

## Pricing hypotheses, not validated willingness to pay

Keep existing prices for the initial measurement window. No existing price was changed by this plan. Start a focused new decision workbook around £7.99–£9.99; consider £12.99–£14.99 only when it contains materially more original templates, cases and end-to-end decisions. These are proposed test prices, not competitor benchmarks. Preserve GBP unless the owner separately requests a coordinated currency migration; do not label a GBP charge as USD.

The higher current prices (£13.99–£18.99) especially need evidence of usefulness and a distinct workflow. Test one price or package change at a time after sufficient comparable traffic; do not silently raise every price. Do not compare all 20 books as though their audiences and traffic quality were equal.

## Distribution and measurement

Today's first companion article is `blog/is-home-energy-audit-worth-it.html`, linked from the blog index and the energy product page. It gives a free scope-comparison table and original worked examples, then explains the paid guide's additional workflow. Internal links and a sitemap make the content discoverable; they do not guarantee indexing, rankings or viral distribution.

Added generic interest measurement to all current product pages through the existing GA tag: `book_detail_view`, `book_preview_click`, `book_checkout_click` and `book_article_click`, with book slug and clean path. QA uses `hce_qa=1` and logs locally. Input values, email, payment data and checkout-session IDs are not included in these custom events. Existing roof/concrete events remain separate: do not add them to the new event counts. These events require the existing tag and permitted analytics to operate; blockers or consent can create missing data. Preview-click counts are not proof of reading.

Use GA4 with country=United States to review qualified product sessions and traffic sources. Use paid, complete **live-mode Stripe orders**, less refunds, as the source for sales and net revenue. One checkout click is not one checkout session or a purchase. Do not equate the new event counts with unique people. Join analysis at product/cohort level unless a separate consent-aware attribution design is implemented.

For each book, review weekly: U.S. product sessions, preview engagement, checkout clicks, paid orders, refunds, net revenue, acquisition source and feedback. Record numerator and denominator, not percentages alone. Diagnose: low relevant traffic → test intent/discovery; views but weak preview activity → revise offer and sample; checkout interest without paid orders → inspect checkout/currency/trust; orders with poor usefulness feedback → improve the book.

For an initial low-traffic heuristic, 100 qualified product visitors with zero sales can justify investigating the offer; it does not prove no demand (rough rule-of-three upper bound is about 3% under simple independent trials). Three sales are an early signal, not validation. Compare predeclared windows with similar traffic and show uncertainty; a formal price experiment needs a separate sample-size plan.

For usefulness, ask buyers to complete one book task and describe the artifact they produced, what remained confusing and whether the purchase met expectations. Existing neutral feedback invitation may support this; do not claim interviews happened or send extra messages without authorization. Seek 5–10 real reader tests per priority title and identify the sample as small. Obtain permission before publishing any real review. No invented testimonials, endorsements or outcomes.
